Sixty seconds of history

1974the year Umberto Deuterio opened the doors

Umberto Deuterio founded Galleria Umberto in 1974, and today his sons Paul and Ralph run it the same way: a short menu, honest prices, and a room that hasn't tried to become something else.

The kitchen turns out a few things and turns them out well. Sicilian pizza with a thick, airy crust and caramelized cheese edges. Arancini, rice balls stuffed with ground beef, peas and mozzarella. Panzarotti, fried golden. Calzones for the table. It is regularly named one of the best bang-for-your-buck lunches in Boston.

Look up when you're on Hanover Street. The building stands where the New Brick Church stood until the 1840s. Locals called it the Cockerel Church for the rooster weathervane on its roof, designed by Shem Drowne — the same smith who made the grasshopper on Faneuil Hall.

The board

It's a short menu on purpose. Everything below comes out of the same ovens, on paper plates, at counter prices. Ask at the register for today's trays; cash only.

Sicilian Pizza

Thick crust cut into squares, with the caramelized cheese edges people cross the city for.

Arancini

Rice balls stuffed with ground beef, peas and mozzarella, fried crisp.

Panzarotti

Fried potato croquettes, golden and salted, gone first most days.

Pizzette

Small round personal pizzas, baked to order.

Calzones

Folded and baked; regulars swear by the spinach, cheese and sausage.

Sodas & drinks

Cold bottles at counter prices, still under two dollars.

Cash only. There's no card machine, and nobody has ever been upset about the price.
